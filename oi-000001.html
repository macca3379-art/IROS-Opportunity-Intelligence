Prediction Market Infrastructure

Where the institutional infrastructure gap is emerging as event-contract markets move toward professional trading — and where commercial value may be captured.

HIGHEvidence strength
HIGHInstitutional relevance
EMERGINGMarket maturity

Executive verdict

Prediction markets are developing from consumer-facing forecasting products into a more institutional trading category. The strongest signal is the convergence of rapid volume growth, professional liquidity participation, increasingly sophisticated institutional access, and explicit infrastructure gaps.

IROS conclusion: the infrastructure opportunity is real enough to investigate commercially, but “prediction-market infrastructure” is still a market thesis rather than one validated business. The next value-creation step is identifying the specific problems with the strongest evidence of institutional pain and willingness to pay.

What changed?

Wintermute confirmed in May 2026 that it had entered prediction markets as a liquidity provider, quoting two-sided markets across leading venues. Wintermute described the segment as having more than $20B of monthly trading volume at the time and emphasized the need for sustained liquidity. Source.

In July 2026, Wintermute Ventures went further, arguing that prediction markets had found product-market fit before the infrastructure was ready. It identified thin books, high fees and trader-unfriendly APIs and explained its investment in Pascal to address the exchange infrastructure gap. Source.

At the venue level, Kalshi now markets institutional access, 8,000+ live markets, 800+ institutional clients and $500B+ annualized volume; these figures are company-reported. Source. Polymarket's institutional proposition includes REST/WebSocket access, real-time streaming, drop copy, VPC connectivity and portfolio-margin capabilities. Source.

Why does this create an opportunity?

Professional market participants require a different infrastructure layer from ordinary users. They need reliable data, fast APIs, liquidity analytics, execution controls, risk systems, settlement clarity and institutional connectivity.

The IROS opportunity mechanism:
Market growth → professional participation → infrastructure bottlenecks → unmet institutional needs → specialized commercial opportunities.

The critical insight is that the opportunity is not necessarily to build another exchange. It may be to build the infrastructure around exchanges.

Where is the opportunity?

1. Cross-venue intelligence

Normalize contracts, probabilities, liquidity, spreads, event metadata and settlement rules across venues. Attractive, but likely to become increasingly commoditized.

2. Institutional data feed

Deliver machine-readable probabilities, depth, volume, event metadata and historical observations. The commercial edge must be interpretation rather than raw data alone.

3. Risk and market-quality intelligence

Measure liquidity-adjusted confidence, probability volatility, concentration, cross-venue disagreement, settlement ambiguity and deterioration in market quality.

4. Institutional market-entry intelligence

Answer which venues matter, where liquidity exists, what infrastructure is required, who the serious players are, what regulatory constraints apply and what should be built versus bought.

5. Opportunity monitoring

Continuously detect new infrastructure gaps, institutional moves, vendor positioning and commercial openings. This is the strongest strategic fit for IROS.

Who could pay?

  • Trading firms: market makers, quantitative firms, hedge funds and proprietary trading firms.
  • Financial institutions: asset managers and other institutions evaluating event-contract exposure or hedging.
  • Infrastructure providers: exchanges, brokers, data vendors, custody, risk and compliance companies.
  • Investors: strategic investors and venture/PE teams mapping the emerging ecosystem.

How could value be captured?

  1. Deep-Dive Opportunity Report — a decision-oriented assessment of one commercial opportunity.
  2. Institutional Dossier — bespoke analysis answering whether and how an institution should enter a market.
  3. Recurring Opportunity Monitoring — continuous alerts when evidence materially changes the opportunity.
  4. Machine-readable Opportunity Feed — structured intelligence for institutional systems and AI agents.

When is the window?

The opportunity window is the transition from rapid adoption to institutional standardization. That is when market participants discover that consumer-grade infrastructure is insufficient, but before every missing layer has become standardized or consolidated into the major venues.

The timing signal is therefore not simply “prediction markets are growing.” It is the simultaneous appearance of professional liquidity, institutional connectivity, infrastructure investment and market-integrity requirements.

What could kill the thesis?

  • Volume falls materially after major event cycles.
  • Institutions do not become recurring participants.
  • Venues solve infrastructure gaps internally.
  • Regulation materially restricts institutional access.
  • Professional economics remain unattractive after fees, liquidity and execution costs.
  • The market consolidates so aggressively that third-party infrastructure has little room.
  • Customers show interest but not willingness to pay.

IROS verdict

Strong market signal. Real institutional infrastructure gap. Specific commercial opportunity still requires validation.

The most promising IROS entry is not building a prediction-market exchange. It is identifying and continuously monitoring the commercial gaps created as the market institutionalizes.

Next investigation: rank the top 3–5 infrastructure problems by institutional pain, willingness to pay, competition, regulatory risk and ease of entry.
