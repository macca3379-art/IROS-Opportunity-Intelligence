Institutional Tokenization Infrastructure

As financial institutions move tokenized assets toward production, the opportunity is shifting from proving tokenization works to building the infrastructure required for scale, interoperability, compliance and institutional operations.

HIGHStructural signal
HIGHInstitutional relevance
DEVELOPINGMarket maturity

Executive verdict

Tokenization is moving from experimentation toward institutional deployment. The important commercial opportunity is increasingly the infrastructure surrounding tokenized assets: issuance, distribution, custody, interoperability, settlement, compliance, identity, data and integration with existing financial systems.

IROS conclusion: the market signal is strong, but the opportunity is fragmented. The most attractive independent opportunities are likely to sit where institutional tokenization collides with legacy operational infrastructure and where interoperability or compliance creates persistent friction.

What changed?

BlackRock's BUIDL tokenized fund has become a prominent institutional example of tokenized real-world assets. Securitize reports that BUIDL surpassed $2B in assets under management in 2025 and expanded to multiple blockchain networks. These are issuer/platform-reported figures.

JPMorgan's Kinexys platform has continued developing institutional digital-asset and tokenized-deposit infrastructure, while major financial-market institutions have continued exploring tokenized funds, deposits and settlement.

The important change is therefore not “blockchain is coming.” It is that established financial institutions are increasingly integrating tokenized instruments into real financial workflows.

Why does this create an opportunity?

Traditional financial institutions already have complex systems for custody, compliance, accounting, reporting, collateral, settlement and client servicing. Tokenization introduces a new representation of assets without eliminating those requirements.

The IROS opportunity mechanism:
Institutional tokenization → new digital asset representations → legacy-system friction + interoperability requirements → infrastructure gaps → specialized commercial opportunities.

The largest opportunity may therefore be in the “plumbing” between tokenized assets and existing institutional infrastructure.

Where is the opportunity?

1. Tokenized-asset interoperability

Connect assets, chains, custodians, transfer agents, venues and institutional systems without forcing institutions onto a single technology stack.

2. Institutional tokenization data

Provide normalized information on tokenized funds, securities, deposits, ownership, liquidity, settlement status and underlying asset characteristics.

3. Compliance and identity infrastructure

Support permissioning, investor eligibility, KYC/AML controls, transfer restrictions and auditable ownership or transaction records.

4. Collateral and settlement intelligence

Track how tokenized assets can be used as collateral and where settlement becomes faster, more transparent or more programmable.

5. Institutional integration

Bridge tokenized products into portfolio management, accounting, treasury, risk and reporting workflows.

Who could pay?

  • Asset managers issuing or distributing tokenized funds.
  • Banks developing tokenized deposits or settlement networks.
  • Custodians and transfer agents.
  • Market infrastructures and exchanges.
  • Institutional investors adopting tokenized assets.
  • Technology and compliance providers serving financial institutions.

How could value be captured?

  1. Tokenization Intelligence — map institutional adoption, products, infrastructure and competitive moves.
  2. Institutional Tokenization Dossier — assess a firm's entry strategy, partners, technology and market opportunities.
  3. Tokenized Asset Data Feed — structured intelligence across products, issuers, chains and institutional activity.
  4. Interoperability / integration services — connect tokenized assets with existing institutional systems.
  5. Opportunity Monitoring — detect new institutional use cases, partnerships, regulatory changes and infrastructure gaps.

When?

The opportunity window is the transition from proof-of-concept to repeatable institutional production. Early tokenization projects prove technical feasibility. The next phase requires standardization, interoperability, operational integration and scalable compliance.

This creates a potentially attractive period for infrastructure providers because institutions have committed to the direction but the operating model is not yet fully standardized.

What could kill the thesis?

  • Tokenized assets remain niche products rather than mainstream institutional instruments.
  • Large banks and market infrastructures build most infrastructure internally.
  • Interoperability standards converge so quickly that independent middleware is commoditized.
  • Regulatory fragmentation slows deployment.
  • Secondary-market liquidity remains too limited to create meaningful economic value.

IROS verdict

Strong institutional structural signal. Large potential addressable ecosystem. Specific independent commercial gaps require further validation.

The strongest IROS angle is the intelligence layer: identify where institutions are actually moving, which infrastructure gaps repeatedly appear, which vendors are gaining traction, and where the next commercially valuable bottleneck is likely to form.

Next investigation: map the tokenization stack and rank the highest-value gaps by institutional spending, competitive crowding, regulatory complexity and time-to-market.
